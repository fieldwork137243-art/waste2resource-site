Benefit-case analysis

Technical possibility is only the start of the value case.

Tailings valorisation can create revenue, avoid lifecycle costs and support closure—but only when the material, process, market and remaining liability work together. This framework helps mining companies screen where value may exist and what evidence is still needed.

Revenue + avoided costRisk + closureMaterial reuse and residual management

Why investigate

Five ways a pathway may create value.

A benefit case must look beyond product revenue. It tests whether valorisation can also reduce lifecycle cost, preserve storage capacity, improve closure outcomes or replace materials already purchased by the operation. These are potential value channels, not assumed benefits.

01

Recover value

Metals, minerals or specification-compliant co-products.

02

Avoid cost

Reduce storage, handling, water, monitoring or closure expenditure.

03

Preserve capacity

Extend storage life or defer infrastructure where diversion is material.

04

Reduce exposure

Support rehabilitation and better land or water outcomes.

05

Create strategic value

Support local supply, reduce purchased material and develop an outlet for a controlled co-product.

Benefit versus constraint

Every value claim needs a corresponding test.

Potential valueEvidence requiredCommon value leak
Product valueYield, specification, customer qualification, price and dependable demandVariable quality, a small market or high transport cost
Avoided lifecycle costA defined baseline, tonnes genuinely diverted and a changed storage or closure planCosts are claimed as avoided while liability remains substantially unchanged
Environmental and strategic valueComparative lifecycle evidence, local demand and measurable outcomesProcessing or haulage transfers impact rather than reducing it

Potential value
  • Margin on a qualified co-product
  • Tonnes diverted from storage and handling
  • Capacity or capital deferral
  • Reduced purchase of on-site material
Costs and obligations
  • Separation, dewatering and quality control
  • Testing and customer qualification
  • Haulage and market development
  • Fines, rejects, water and residual liability
Inputs still required
  • Representative yield and variability
  • Demand, specification, price and distance
  • Baseline cost genuinely avoided
  • CAPEX, OPEX, approvals and liability

Screening equation: product margin + avoided handling/storage + deferred capital + avoided imported material − processing − assurance − logistics − residual management.

Use the equation as a structure for evidence, not as proof of feasibility.

Circular and site-specific

Use more of the material already disturbed, and plan for the remainder.

The objective is not to find any use for a waste stream. It is to place suitable material in its highest practicable use, reduce additional extraction and improve the final condition of what remains.

Material and process

Are samples representative, and are yield, variability, water, energy and residuals demonstrated at relevant scale?

Market and logistics

Who needs the output, against which specification, at what volume, price and haul distance?

Mine plan and economics

Does diversion change storage, purchasing, closure or infrastructure decisions after CAPEX and OPEX?

Risk and approvals

Who retains liability, and what assurance, approvals, monitoring and contingency remain?

Decision rule: compare the pathway with a defined business-as-usual baseline. Do not count revenue or circularity without the costs and obligations required to make, qualify, move and manage the product and residuals.
