About Waste2Resource.

An independent knowledge platform built around a practical question about the future of tailings.

Why I started Waste2Resource

It began with a passing question.

After my first visit to a mine site, I found myself explaining my work to my dad. I described what tailings are and why they are stored in a tailings storage facility. Somewhere in that conversation, we casually wondered whether the material could be reused.

It was a light comment, but the question stayed with me. I began reading the research, discussing it with people around me and contacting authors whose work showed that tailings reuse was technically possible. I kept asking: if it can be demonstrated in theory, what is stopping it from happening in practice?

The answers were remarkably consistent: transport distances, economics, scale, regulation and the challenge of connecting a variable material with the right end use. The evidence suggested real potential, but the path to implementation still felt distant.

That perspective is beginning to change. Mining is responding to decarbonisation, sustainable closure and circular-economy expectations. As this happens, more people are considering tailings as a potential resource rather than only as material to manage. Waste2Resource brings together the evidence and the people trying to turn it into practical work.

“What began as a passing question became an ongoing engineering inquiry.”

About me

I am a curious engineer who enjoys working through complex technical problems and looking for practical, innovative solutions. I am especially interested in questions that sit between disciplines. These are places where sound engineering, environmental responsibility and workable project delivery must align.

My experience across tailings, infrastructure, project delivery and public-sector work has taught me that a promising idea needs more than technical merit. It also needs context, evidence, collaboration and a realistic path to implementation. That way of thinking shapes how Waste2Resource is researched and developed.

About the platform

Waste2Resource is an independent, engineering-led knowledge platform. It brings together source-linked research, operating examples and a practical assessment framework for tailings reuse, mine-waste valorisation and circular infrastructure. The aim is to help practitioners find relevant evidence while keeping its assumptions, uncertainties and limitations attached.

Editorial independence matters. Entries are selected and summarised to show the evidence, uncertainty and limits on applying results elsewhere. They do not endorse a company, technology or product. Waste2Resource is provided for information and professional discussion; it does not replace site investigation, design, approvals or project-specific engineering advice.
