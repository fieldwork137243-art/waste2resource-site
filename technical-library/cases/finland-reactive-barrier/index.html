Tailings-containing adsorbents screened for mine-water treatment

Mine-tailings-containing alkali-activated adsorbents were screened in laboratory tests, but the field pilot used a slag-only formulation.

Northern FinlandLaboratory screeningLaboratory evidence
Source states:

Mine-tailings-containing adsorbents were screened in laboratory work, but the one-week field pilot used the selected slag-only adsorbent rather than a tailings-containing formulation.

Verify in International Journal of Environmental Science and Technology ↗

What was developed or tested

The study compared metakaolin, slag, mine tailings and zeolite formulations. The selected 0.5 m³/day field-pilot material contained blast-furnace slag only, so it did not demonstrate tailings reuse at pilot scale.

What the evidence supports

The study supports continued laboratory investigation of tailings-containing adsorbents. It does not support a claim that tailings-derived media were proven in the field pilot.

Environmental and technical uncertainty

Media stability, saturation, regeneration or disposal and release of bound constituents govern lifecycle performance.

Benefit-case screening lens

Potential value: Potential value includes water recovery, a reduced discharge burden and support for compliance. Some projects may also recover metals or useful treatment products when the evidence supports that outcome.

Deployment constraints: Flow and chemistry variability, consumables, energy, plant reliability, sludge or spent-media management and long-term treatment obligations.

Question before applying elsewhere

Can treatment performance, media life, hydraulic behaviour and spent-material management be demonstrated under representative field conditions?
