Technical case record · Construction

Mina do Pico tailings block factory

Vale operates a block plant that converts sandy iron-ore tailings into interlocking pavers and other precast products.

Itabirito, Minas Gerais, BrazilOperating demonstrationOperating evidence
Source states:

Vale reports that the Pico plant has converted tailings into interlocking pavers since 2021 and produced more than 407,000 pavers in 2025.

Verify in Vale project announcement ↗

What was developed or tested

The plant converts silica-rich iron-ore tailings into precast products. Vale reported more than 407,000 interlocking pavers produced during 2025.

What the evidence supports

A current industrial demonstration of tailings entering conventional construction products. The production figures are operator-reported and have not been independently verified here.

Environmental and technical uncertainty

Substitution for natural sand and avoided storage are potential benefits. Public disclosure does not replace independent product, leaching or lifecycle verification.

Benefit-case screening lens

Potential value: Potential co-product revenue, reduced demand for virgin aggregate or binder inputs, local supply and avoided tailings storage.

Deployment constraints: Product specification, consistency, processing and binder demand, customer qualification, market volume, haulage distance and residual liability.

Question before applying elsewhere

Does the site-specific residue meet durability, leaching, production-control and applicable material-specification requirements at the proposed scale?
