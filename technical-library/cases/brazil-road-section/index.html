Iron tailings in an experimental pavement section

Tailings, waste rock and iron-ore canga were placed in base and sub-base mixtures in a monitored experimental road section.

Congonhas, Minas Gerais, BrazilField demonstrationDemonstration evidence
Source states:

The 35% tailings mixtures behaved differently in the field, and cracking and deformation appeared within a year; the authors recommend more conservative proportions.

Verify in REM: International Engineering Journal (2022) ↗

What was developed or tested

Laboratory validation informed two field mixtures: 35% tailings with waste rock and canga in the base, and 35% tailings with graded aggregates in the sub-base. Field response exposed deformation sensitivity in the sub-base.

What the evidence supports

A valuable field-scale counterpoint to laboratory pavement studies. The trial demonstrated constructability while identifying sub-base deformation as a limiting behaviour requiring mix and layer redesign.

Environmental and technical uncertainty

Large-volume road use can displace quarried material, but drainage, dust, geochemistry and long-term deformation must be controlled. The field result shows why laboratory compliance alone is insufficient.

Benefit-case screening lens

Potential value: Potential co-product revenue, reduced demand for virgin aggregate or binder inputs, local supply and avoided tailings storage.

Deployment constraints: Product specification, consistency, processing and binder demand, customer qualification, market volume, haulage distance and residual liability.

Question before applying elsewhere

Does the site-specific residue meet durability, leaching, production-control and applicable material-specification requirements at the proposed scale?
