Nador iron tailings in fired clay bricks

Iron-mine tailings from an inactive operation were evaluated as an additive in red-clay fired bricks.

Nador, MoroccoLaboratoryLaboratory evidence
Source states:

Specimens containing 60% tailings reached 1.25 MPa compressive strength and had greater porosity than the reference bricks.

Verify in University of Liège research record ↗

What was developed or tested

The study characterised a 60% tailings and 40% red-clay blend and fired specimens at 500°C, 850°C and 1100°C. Tailings specimens reached only 1.25 MPa compressive strength and remained more porous than the reference.

What the evidence supports

The study demonstrates that the blend can be formed and fired, but the reported strength and porosity do not establish a specification-ready brick. Further formulation work is required.

Environmental and technical uncertainty

Firing can immobilise some constituents but introduces thermal energy and emissions; leaching and kiln-specific performance require verification.

Benefit-case screening lens

Potential value: Potential co-product revenue, reduced demand for virgin aggregate or binder inputs, local supply and avoided tailings storage.

Deployment constraints: Product specification, consistency, processing and binder demand, customer qualification, market volume, haulage distance and residual liability.

Question before applying elsewhere

Does the site-specific residue meet durability, leaching, production-control and applicable material-specification requirements at the proposed scale?
