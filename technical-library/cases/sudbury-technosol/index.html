Technical case record · Land restoration

Sudbury amended-tailings Technosols

Mine residuals were blended with pulp-and-paper residues and forest humus to create plant-growth substrates.

Sudbury, Ontario, CanadaGrowth-chamber studyConcept evidence
Source states:

Organic amendments increased pH, germination and plant growth, but soluble copper and nickel also increased in some amended substrates.

Verify in Science of the Total Environment / PubMed ↗

What was developed or tested

The study measured substrate chemistry, microbial communities, germination and biomass in both acid-producing and neutral-to-alkaline tailings.

What the evidence supports

A circular rehabilitation concept combining regional residual streams. Controlled work supports biological feasibility; field hydrology and long-term geochemistry remain unresolved.

Environmental and technical uncertainty

Organic amendments improved nutrient status and raised pH in acid tailings, but field persistence, supply and contaminant uptake require verification.

Benefit-case screening lens

Potential value: Closure and rehabilitation outcomes, reduced erosion or exposure, improved land use and possible avoidance of imported rehabilitation materials.

Deployment constraints: Long-term geochemistry, soil function, vegetation performance, water quality, monitoring duration and whether treatment changes the underlying liability.

Question before applying elsewhere

Will the amended material sustain vegetation while controlling erosion, pore-water quality and contaminant exposure over the required timeframe?
